# 01 — Brand & Voice

## 1. Brand Identity

### Name

Muhammed Al-Ateeqi

The personal name should be clearly visible throughout the website.

This is a personal brand website, not a product/company website.

The website should be designed so that when someone searches for "Muhammed Al-Ateeqi", this website can become the primary destination associated with his professional identity.

---

## 2. Core Identity

Muhammed should not be presented as "just a Frontend Developer."

His engineering background is the professional foundation, but his identity is broader.

He naturally moves between:

- Software Engineering
- Problem Solving
- Product Thinking
- Business
- Entrepreneurship
- Content Creation
- Teaching
- Self-Learning

The website should communicate that these are not random unrelated interests.

They are different expressions of the same underlying mindset:

> Understand the problem, see the bigger picture, and build something useful.

---

## 3. Professional Positioning

### Primary positioning

Software Engineer & Builder

### Supporting identity

A software engineer who looks beyond the code to understand problems, processes, people, and business needs — then turns them into practical solutions.

This should remain flexible.

We should NOT force a long list of titles such as:

> Software Engineer · Entrepreneur · Content Creator · Educator · Product Builder · Business Owner

everywhere on the website.

The work should prove these dimensions naturally.

---

## 4. Personal Philosophy

A recurring idea throughout the website:

> Don't just build the thing.
> Understand why it needs to exist.

Muhammed's approach starts with understanding the full process before choosing a technical implementation.

He tends to:

1. Understand the current process.
2. Identify friction and unnecessary work.
3. Understand who is affected.
4. Brainstorm multiple possible solutions.
5. Compare trade-offs.
6. Choose an appropriate solution.
7. Build it.
8. Observe the result.
9. Improve it.

This philosophy is important because it separates the portfolio from a collection of technical demos.

---

## 5. One-Line Identity

The website needs a short statement that answers:

> "Who is Muhammed?"

**Headline**

> Building practical software for problems I live with

**Subline**

> I'm Muhammed, a software engineer and builder. I'm usually the one stuck in the problem myself — so I scope it, build the interface, wire it to real data, and ship it end to end.

Why this wording carries the brand: it communicates all four required signals — software engineering, building, problem solving, bigger-picture thinking — without asserting any of them as an adjective. "Problems I live with" is evidence-shaped rather than claim-shaped (per §9, Rule 1: evidence over adjectives), and it is literally true of the project set: the Nutella menu came from being a customer, the ST portal from watching onboarding fail around him. The subline's verb sequence ("scope it, build the interface, wire it to real data, ship it") is the §4 process made concrete rather than listed as a numbered philosophy.

# 9. Writing Rules

## Rule 1 — Evidence over adjectives

Avoid:

> "I am a highly passionate and innovative developer."

Prefer:

> "I noticed the team was repeatedly searching across spreadsheets and messaging apps, so I built one operational dashboard."

---

## Rule 2 — Explain the WHY

Every important project should answer:

> Why did this need to exist?

Technology should come after the problem.

---

## Rule 3 — Keep technical credibility

The website should not hide technical details.

However, technical information should be contextual.

For example:

Bad:

> Angular, TypeScript, Firebase, RxJS, Tailwind, REST APIs...

Better:

> I built the application with Angular and Firebase because the system was relatively small, serverless was sufficient, and I needed to move quickly without introducing unnecessary infrastructure.

---

## Rule 4 — No fake authority

Never claim:

- Senior-level expertise unless supported
- Leadership that did not happen
- Metrics that were not measured
- Business outcomes that cannot be verified
- Technologies that were not actually used

---

## Rule 5 — No unnecessary humility

Do not write:

> "I'm just a junior developer trying to learn."

The website should accurately communicate current capability while remaining honest about growth areas.

>

> How does he think?

>

# 11. The Two-Layer Experience

The website intentionally contains two layers.

## Layer 1 — Professional World

This is the primary experience.

It contains:

- Software Engineering
- Projects
- Case Studies
- Experience
- Skills
- Education
- Certifications
- Resume
- Contact

This layer must work independently.

A recruiter should be able to visit the website, understand Muhammed professionally, and leave without exploring anything else.

---

## Layer 2 — The World Behind the Engineer

This is the discovery layer.

It can contain:

- Content Creation
- Social Media
- Entrepreneurship
- Business
- Teaching
- Personal growth
- The journey behind the career

This layer should feel like discovering another side of the same person.

The desired reaction is:

> "I thought I was looking at a software engineer's portfolio. There is much more behind this person."

---

# 12. Progressive Disclosure

The website should NOT force the second world onto every visitor.

Instead:

Professional information is immediately visible.

Personal depth is progressively revealed.

Example:

Hero
↓
Work
↓
Case Study
↓
Story
↓
Beyond Code
↓
Social / Business

The further someone explores, the more they understand Muhammed as a person.

> Computer → Gaming → Video Editing → Content Creation → Programming → Computer Science → Freelancing → Business → Leadership → Setback → Rebuilding → Software Engineering → Technical Growth

The important theme is not the individual activities.

The computer was present throughout the journey, while Muhammed's role kept changing:

> User → Creator → Freelancer → Business Owner → Programmer → Leader → Software Engineer

This should eventually become one of the site's strongest narrative elements.

---

# 21. Animation Philosophy

Animation exists to support storytelling.

It should NOT exist simply because animation is possible.

Preferred:

- subtle section reveals
- meaningful transitions
- image movement
- hover interactions
- small navigation transitions
- storytelling sequences

Avoid:

- excessive parallax
- scroll hijacking
- constant movement
- giant entrance animations
- cursor gimmicks
- animation everywhere

All motion should respect:

`prefers-reduced-motion`

---

The website should not try to convince visitors that Muhammed is extraordinary.

It should make the visitor discover it through the evidence.

The strongest message should come from:

- projects
- decisions
- outcomes

- business experience
- audience
- technical work

not from adjectives.
